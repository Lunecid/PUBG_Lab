\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{What the model sees matters.} With the learner held fixed (gradient boosting), the representations of Sagong et al. and of this paper differ by 0.10 AUC, so the gap lies in the input representation, not in model capacity \todo{K2: say whether the KRAFTON gap has the same source}. \emph{The effect of structure depends on data.} The benefit of phase history changes sign with cohort size (slope +0.0025 per $\ln N$, crossover near 590 matches), and the attention model overtakes gradient boosting near 700 matches; the benefit of cross-team attention does not depend on size. Small cohorts therefore favour gradient boosting and large cohorts the attention model. \emph{Relation graphs do not go beyond attention.} Proximity, combat, and zone co-pressure biases are equivalent to shuffled relations (P4), and teammate relations were equivalent as well; we read this as learned cross-team attention already holding this information. \emph{Cross-team information matters more for relative standing.} The team-axis gain is larger in Spearman (P3, +0.0094) than in AUC (P2, +0.0033), which fits the view that the benefit of KRAFTON-style attention lies in relative standing more than in absolute risk. \emph{A learned object does not replace hand-crafted spatial features.} The safe-zone object recovers $\kappa=0.29$ of their information, so domain-informed feature design remains necessary.

\emph{Limitations.} The data cover a single game (PUBG), public matches, and a patch window from March to June 2026, without a temporal hold-out. The external baselines are adapted implementations, not reproductions: for KRAFTON, some inputs could not be reconstructed and the original target is win probability; for Sagong et al., the player- and half-phase-level method was aggregated to teams and full phases. The data-scale analysis is post hoc: the meta-regression is confounded with map and mode differences, and the learning curves cover two cohorts only. Apart from P1--P5, results are exploratory and uncorrected. AI accounts remain in official matches; we give each team's AI share to the model and report results by match type.

\emph{Conclusion.} On audited event-based labels, what a model sees matters more than how it relates teams over time: a phase-conditioned representation of zone and local-competition context scores 0.06--0.10 AUC above adapted implementations of earlier approaches, whereas phase history, explicit relations, and teammate relations add little on average, cross-team attention adds a small but consistent gain, and structure pays off only in cohorts with roughly 700 or more training matches. Next steps are a temporal hold-out on newly collected matches and win-probability heads.
