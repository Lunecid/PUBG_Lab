\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\emph{Versus adapted implementations of earlier approaches.} On identical rows, implementations of Sagong et al. adapted to our task reach a mean AUC of 0.690 (XGBoost), 0.676 (logistic regression), and 0.656 (random forest), against 0.794 for gradient boosting on our representation (HGB-cut) and for M11R (Table~\ref{tab:external}). M11R is +0.103 AUC [0.096, 0.109] and +0.229 Spearman [0.211, 0.246] above SAGONG-xgb in 12 of 12 cohorts, and HGB-cut is +0.102 [0.095, 0.108] above it. With the learner family held fixed (gradient boosting), the 0.10 AUC gap thus separates the two input representations, not model capacity. KRAFTON's official code adapted to our task reaches 0.723 (transformer, 9 cohorts) and 0.708 (MLP encoder, 8 cohorts) \todo{refresh both rows after the aggregator re-run covers all cohorts}; M11R is +0.057 AUC [0.050, 0.064] above KRAFTON-tf (9 of 9 cohorts), whereas the transformer and the MLP encoder differ by +0.012 [$-$0.003, 0.026] (8 cohorts; inconclusive). On rank metrics M11R $-$ KRAFTON-tf is +0.015 [$-$0.010, 0.040] in Spearman and +0.006 [$-$0.005, 0.017] in C-index (both inconclusive), while KRAFTON-tf is higher in top-5 AUC ($-$0.067 [$-$0.096, $-$0.038]), which may reflect its win-probability target. \todo{K2: report KRAFTON-tf-ourfeat and state whether the KRAFTON gap lies in the input representation or in the backbone.}

\emph{Pre-specified decomposition (RQ1--RQ4).} Table~\ref{tab:primary} and Fig.~\ref{fig:forest} give the five primary contrasts. Phase history adds nothing meaningful to hazard AUC (P1: +0.0007 [$-$0.0013, 0.0027], Holm $p=0.24$, 7 of 12 cohorts positive; equivalent), and explicit relations are equivalent to shuffled relations (P4: +0.0005 [$-$0.0001, 0.0010], Holm $p=0.08$). Cross-team attention gives a small but highly consistent gain, P2 +0.0033 AUC [0.0024, 0.0042] (Holm $p<0.001$, 10 of 12 cohorts) and P3 +0.0094 Spearman [0.0045, 0.0143] (Holm $p<0.001$, 9 of 12), reliable but below the SESOI. The safe-zone object adds +0.0032 AUC [0.0012, 0.0051] when the hand-crafted zone and habitat features are removed (P5: Holm $p=0.002$, reliable but small), which recovers $\kappa=0.29$ [0.19, 0.39] of the +0.0111 [0.0095, 0.0127] that these features carry. Exploratory contrasts agree: M11R $-$ M11 is +0.0007 [0.0002, 0.0013]; dropping one relation type changes AUC by +0.0004 (proximity), +0.0021 (combat), and $-$0.0003 (zone co-pressure) in the two largest cohorts; and adding the object to the full feature set changes AUC by $-$0.0010 [$-$0.0021, 0.0001]. In competitive matches (6 cohorts) P1 and P5 are larger (+0.006 and +0.009 AUC, exploratory), and official matches reproduce the pooled results.

\emph{Data scale (additional analysis, exploratory).} Fig.~\ref{fig:lc} shows learning curves on the two largest cohorts. At 300 training matches HGB-cut is ahead of M11R (Erangel $-$0.006 AUC, Taego $-$0.010), the two meet between 600 and 1,200 matches, and M11R leads on the full training sets (+0.010 in both cohorts). Phase history follows the same pattern: M10 is below M00 at 300 matches and above it at the full size. Across the 12 cohorts (189 to 3,329 training matches), the advantage of M11R over HGB-cut grows by +0.0067 AUC per $\ln N$ [0.0044, 0.0090] and crosses zero near 700 matches [424, 964] (Table~\ref{tab:metareg}); the phase-axis main effect grows by +0.0025 per $\ln N$ and crosses near 590 matches [206, 983], whereas the team-axis effect does not depend on cohort size (slope +0.0002, $p=0.77$).
