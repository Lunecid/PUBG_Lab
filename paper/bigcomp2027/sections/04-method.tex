\section{PTR-Former and the Arm Ladder}\label{sec:method}

PTR-Former reads, for every phase of a match, a small graph of team nodes, one safe-zone object node, and typed relations. It applies relation-biased attention along the team axis and causal attention along the phase axis, and two heads read a next-phase hazard and a within-match standing score from the result. Each component is a switch, and all arms share the backbone, the loss, and the training setup. We drop the match index $m$ in this section.

\emph{Stage-1 graph.} For each phase $k$ the graph has four parts; all node features and edges use samples taken no later than 10 s before the phase cut. \emph{Team nodes} are the teams alive at the start of the phase, each with its 150-d vector $\mathbf{x}_{i,k}$ and normalised 2-D centroid $\mathbf{c}_{i,k}$ at the phase boundary. The \emph{safe-zone object node} $z_k$ carries raw zone geometry only, $\mathbf{u}_k$: centre $\mathbf{o}_k$ and radius $\rho_k$ of the current zone, centre $\mathbf{o}'_k$ and radius $\rho'_k$ of the next zone, the elapsed fraction of the phase, and the phase number. Processed quantities such as boundary distance or required speed are left out on purpose: RQ4 asks whether the model recovers them. The \emph{team--team relations} are \texttt{prox} (symmetric), \texttt{combat\_out} (attacker to victim), \texttt{combat\_in} (its transpose), and \texttt{copress} (symmetric), with the raw weights of Section~\ref{sec:data} and no row normalisation. Combat replaces proximity for a pair that exchanged damage in the phase (escalation), and combat biases are learned separately from the proximity bias: with $E_{ij}=1$ if the pair exchanged damage in either direction, $W_{\mathrm{prox}}=(1-E)\odot w^{\mathrm{prox}}$, $W_{\mathrm{out}}=w^{\mathrm{comb}}$, $W_{\mathrm{in}}=(w^{\mathrm{comb}})^{\top}$, and $W_{\mathrm{cop}}=w^{\mathrm{copress}}$. The \emph{team--object edges} are continuous biases from the position of the team centroid relative to the zone:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathbf{g}_{i,k}=\Bigl[&\tfrac{\mathbf{c}_{i,k}-\mathbf{o}_k}{\rho_k},\ \tfrac{\|\mathbf{c}_{i,k}-\mathbf{o}_k\|}{\rho_k},\\
&\tfrac{\mathbf{c}_{i,k}-\mathbf{o}'_k}{\rho'_k},\ \tfrac{\|\mathbf{c}_{i,k}-\mathbf{o}'_k\|}{\rho'_k}\Bigr]\in\R^{6},
\end{split}
\label{eq:geom}
\end{equation}
the offset and distance to the current and the next zone, scaled by the zone radius. A two-layer MLP, $6\to16\to H$, one per block, maps $\mathbf{g}_{i,k}$ to one bias $\beta_h(\mathbf{g}_{i,k})$ per head.

\emph{Tokens and axes.} Team tokens are $\mathbf{t}_{i,k}=\mathrm{LN}(W_e[\mathbf{x}_{i,k};\mathbf{c}_{i,k}]+\mathbf{b}_e)+\mathbf{p}_k$ and the zone token is $\mathbf{t}_{z,k}=\mathrm{LN}(W_z\mathbf{u}_k+\mathbf{b}_z)+\mathbf{p}_k$, with a learned phase embedding $\mathbf{p}_k\in\R^{d}$; absent teams and phases are masked. Each block applies multi-head attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention} along the phase axis of each team token (the zone token takes no part in this step), then along the team axis within each phase over team tokens and the zone token, then a feed-forward layer, with residual connections and layer normalisation; $L$ blocks are stacked. With the phase switch $s_P\in\{0,1\}$, team token $(i,k)$ attends to phases $l\le k$ if $s_P=1$ and only to $l=k$ if $s_P=0$. Let $a_{j,k}=1$ if team $j$ was alive at the start of phase $k$, and let $s_T$, $s_R$, and $s_Z\in\{0,1\}$ switch the team axis, the relation bias, and the zone object. For head $h$, the logit from query team $i$ to a key team $j$ is
\begin{equation}
e^{(h)}_{ij,k}=\frac{\mathbf{q}^{(h)\top}_{i,k}\mathbf{k}^{(h)}_{j,k}}{\sqrt{d_h}}+s_R\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}b_{r,h}\,W_{r,k}[i,j],
\label{eq:team-logit}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{R}=\{\mathrm{prox},\mathrm{out},\mathrm{in},\mathrm{cop}\}$, and the logit to the zone token is
\begin{equation}
e^{(h)}_{iz,k}=\frac{\mathbf{q}^{(h)\top}_{i,k}\mathbf{k}^{(h)}_{z,k}}{\sqrt{d_h}}+\beta_h(\mathbf{g}_{i,k}).
\label{eq:zone-logit}
\end{equation}
The scalars $b_{r,h}$ are learned per head and block, the diagonal of every $W_r$ is zero, and a team may attend to itself. The zone token is both a query and a key in team-axis attention and is updated in every block: as a query it attends to the alive teams and to itself, with the same bias applied symmetrically, $\beta_h(\mathbf{g}_{j,k})$ on its logit to team $j$.
The weights are a softmax over the allowed keys: with $s_T=1$, the teams with $a_{j,k}=1$ and, if $s_Z=1$, the zone token; with $s_T=0$, the query attends only to itself. The relation bias and the zone token act only inside team-axis attention, so $s_R=1$ and $s_Z=1$ require $s_T=1$. With causal phase attention and team attention within one phase, $\mathbf{z}_{i,k}$, the representation of team $i$ after the last block, depends only on tokens with phase index $\le k$ and therefore respects $\mathcal{F}^m(k)$.

\emph{Heads and loss.} Both heads read $\mathbf{z}_{i,k}$. The hazard head gives $\hat\lambda_{i,k}=\sigma(\mathbf{w}_h^\top\mathbf{z}_{i,k}+c_h)$, and the rank head gives a scalar standing score $s_{i,k}=\mathbf{w}_s^\top\mathbf{z}_{i,k}$ in which larger means a better expected final placement. Let $\Omega$ be the training cases $(m,i,k)$ and $\pi_i$ the final placement of team $i$ (1 is the winner). The loss is
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}&=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{haz}}+\eta\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rank}},\label{eq:loss}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rank}}&=\operatorname*{mean}_{(m,k)}\ \operatorname*{mean}_{\substack{i,j\in\mathcal{R}_m(k)\\ \pi_i<\pi_j}}\log\bigl(1+e^{-(s_{i,k}-s_{j,k})}\bigr),
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{haz}}$ is the binary cross-entropy of $\hat\lambda_{i,k}$ against $Y_i(k{+}1)$ over $\Omega$, the discrete-time survival likelihood over at-risk rows. The pairwise rank loss and its weight $\eta=0.1$ are identical in every arm, so attention changes are never confounded with ranking changes.

\emph{Arms.} Switching the phase axis off restricts attention to the current phase, and switching the team axis off restricts each team to itself (Table~\ref{tab:arms}). M00, M10, M01, and M11 form a 2$\times$2 factorial design over the two axes. All neural arms have the same parameters apart from the $4HL$ relation-bias scalars and the zone-token and bias networks, which are unused when their switches are off. M11R-shuf is retrained with shuffled relations, so its contrast with M11R tests whether the alignment of relations with teams carries information. The noHab arms and HGB-noHab remove the Hab block, the Zone and Habitat families except the two alive-count variables, which stay (45 of the 150 features). HGB-lag adds the previous phase's values and the phase-to-phase differences to HGB-cut (missing at $k=1$), the tabular counterpart of the phase axis.

The RQ4 verdict comes from M11RZ-noHab $-$ M11R-noHab (P5 in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). The recovery ratio
\begin{equation}
\kappa=\frac{\mathrm{AUC}(\text{M11RZ-noHab})-\mathrm{AUC}(\text{M11R-noHab})}{\mathrm{AUC}(\text{M11R})-\mathrm{AUC}(\text{M11R-noHab})}
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
is descriptive: its denominator is the information carried by Hab when no object node is present, and its numerator is what the object node adds once Hab is removed.
