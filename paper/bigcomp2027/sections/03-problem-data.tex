\section{Problem, Data, and Labels}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Phase-conditioned discrete hazard}\label{sec:hazard}
Let $\mathcal{I}_m$ be the teams of match $m$, and let the safe-zone schedule divide the match into phases $k=1,2,\dots$, the discrete clock. The risk set $\mathcal{R}_m(k)\subseteq\mathcal{I}_m$ contains the teams still alive at the end of phase $k$; a team is eliminated when its last member is eliminated, and knocked-down members still count as alive. For $i\in\mathcal{R}_m(k)$ the label is
\begin{equation}
Y_i^m(k{+}1)=\ind{\,i\text{ is eliminated in phase }k{+}1\,},
\label{eq:label}
\end{equation}
and a prediction case is $(m,i,k)$ with $k\in\{1,\dots,7\}$; the winner is censored. The telemetry is a spectator view, so the information $\mathcal{F}^m(k)$ available at the end of phase $k$ comprises the features, centroids, zone geometry, and relations of all teams in phases $l\le k$ (Section~\ref{sec:repr}). The target is the discrete-time hazard
\begin{equation}
\lambda_i^m(k)=\Pr\bigl(Y_i^m(k{+}1)=1\mid\mathcal{F}^m(k)\bigr).
\label{eq:hazard}
\end{equation}
The components studied here restrict what a model reads from $\mathcal{F}^m(k)$ when it scores team $i$: the \emph{phase axis} allows phases $l\le k$ instead of $l=k$, the \emph{team axis} teams $j\neq i$, the \emph{relation} biases the relation matrices, and the \emph{zone object} the raw zone geometry. Section~\ref{sec:method} turns these into switches.

\subsection{Data and population}\label{sec:data}
We use match metadata and telemetry from the official PUBG API, collected between 2026-03-26 and 2026-06-02: 17,943 matches in 13 map, perspective (TPP or FPP), and team-size (solo, duo, squad) cohorts. We exclude \texttt{airoyale} matches, whose lobbies are dominated by bots, and the Vikendi cohort, whose map adds non-player combatants (monsters) that damage players. The 12 remaining cohorts hold 13,948 matches (11,285 official and 2,663 competitive), from 224 (Erangel TPP solo) to 4,159 (Taego TPP squad) per cohort. Each cohort is split at the match level with the stored 80/10/10 split (seed 42), kept for the surviving matches, which gives 11,188, 1,376, and 1,384 training, validation, and test matches (20 to 415 test matches per cohort). For $k=1,\dots,7$ the event-based labels below yield 829,105 risk-set rows, with elimination rates between 24.6\% and 34.8\% per cohort. AI accounts remain in official matches: 15.9\% of teams consist of AI accounts only (none in competitive matches), so we add each team's AI share to its input and report results by match type.

\subsection{Label construction and audit}\label{sec:labels}
An earlier pipeline derived the label from the last position snapshot of a team in each phase: the team counted as alive at the phase end if a healthy observed player was present. Auditing this rule against kill telemetry on all 17,943 collected matches (3.34 M label rows, 1.03 M of them used for training) shows that 11.4\% of the training rows are wrongly included in the risk set or wrongly labelled. The positive precision of the old labels is 70.4\% (recall 75.2\%), and late-phase hazards are underestimated (recall for final-phase eliminations 51\%). Among the rows where the two disagree, the last snapshot holds only knocked-down players in 38.5\%, predates the death in 22.2\%, involves revived or re-deployed teammates in 20.3\%, lies within 15 s of a boundary in 16.6\%, and has no position row in 2.4\% (mutually exclusive error types). On the 13,948 retained matches the rule is wrong for 12.6\% of its risk-set rows (positive precision 65.8\%).

We therefore define a team's elimination time as the latest death event of its members, with survivors at the match end (winners) censored; the risk set at cut $k$ holds the teams whose elimination time exceeds the end of phase $k$, and the label is elimination by the end of phase $k{+}1$ (the final phase extends to the match end). Event times and phase boundaries share the match-start clock, whereas position windows follow an elapsed-time clock offset by a per-match $\delta$ (median 5.7 s, maximum 41.9 s). We estimate $\delta$ per match and align the clocks before applying the 10 s cut of Section~\ref{sec:repr}; otherwise a look-ahead would remain in about 27\% of matches. For human-only teams the event times agree with the official survival times within 5 s for 95.9\% of teams (99.1\% state agreement per row); official times are unusable for AI accounts and revived players and serve only as a check.

\subsection{Representation}\label{sec:repr}
Only the phase tier is used. Per match and phase, a team token carries 150 features (125 team-level and 25 match-level values) in nine families (Table~\ref{tab:features}), a normalised 2-D centroid at the phase boundary, and the team's AI share; the zone geometry (current and next centre and radius, elapsed fraction, phase number) stays raw. The Zone and Habitat families, except the two alive-count variables (recomputed from events), form the 45-feature block called Hab. Three relation types hold raw weights: proximity $\exp(-\bar d_{ij}/200)$ with the mean distance $\bar d_{ij}$ in metres (symmetric); combat $\log(1+D_{ij}+100K_{ij}+50N_{ij})$ with the damage, kills, and knock-downs inflicted by $i$ on $j$ (directed); and zone co-pressure $\tfrac{c_{ij}}{n_{ij}}\exp(-\bar d^{\mathrm{co}}_{ij}/200)$ (symmetric), where $c_{ij}/n_{ij}$ is the share of joint observations in which both teams are within 200 m of each other and outside the zone or within 150 m inside its boundary. All features, centroids, geometry, and edges use samples taken no later than 10 s before the phase cut on the aligned clock. The 10-second snapshot tier is not used.
