\section{Introduction}

Final placement in a battle-royale match is the accumulated result of
survival risks that change as the playable area contracts. In
\textit{PUBG: Battlegrounds}, players land, loot, fight, and survive in a
shrinking battleground; the Blue Zone reduces health when players linger
outside the playable area~\cite{pubgGameInfo}. During a match, safe-area
updates and Blue Zone movement form observable zone-change intervals. We
index these intervals from telemetry and use them as \textit{analysis
phases}, so that survival risk can be measured at comparable points in the
spatial contraction process. Under this representation, the same damage,
travel distance, or enemy encounter can have different meaning depending on
the current zone pressure and safe-area geometry. Post-match aggregates
therefore hide when a team became vulnerable and which spatial condition
shaped that risk.

We study official PUBG telemetry from the team perspective. The developer API
records player positions, combat, zone state, phase-change events, and
eliminations over time
\cite{pubgDeveloperPortal,pubgTelemetryEvents,pubgTelemetryObjects}. We
aggregate player records into team trajectories, exposure, and relations, and
define team elimination as the moment when the last surviving team member is
eliminated. The central task is to predict, from observations available
through the end of indexed interval \(k\), whether a team is eliminated during
the next interval \(k+1\). We call this the \textit{next-phase elimination
hazard}, following the discrete-time event-history view of survival
\cite{allison1982discrete,singer1993discrete}.

The work asks two questions. \textbf{RQ1}: Can official PUBG telemetry be
reconstructed into a phase-aligned STDB from which team trajectories, zone
exposure, combat events, and dynamic team relations are derived as features
and graphs? \textbf{RQ2}: On that representation, how accurately can
feature-only models predict next-phase hazard, and do dynamic graphs add
signal beyond those features? Our contributions are: (1) a team-level,
phase-level hazard formulation for battle-royale survival; (2) a
phase-aligned STDB representation over 21,824 matches; and (3) an empirical
separation of representation gains from graph-relation gains.
