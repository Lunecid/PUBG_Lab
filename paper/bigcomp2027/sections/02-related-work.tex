\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\emph{Battle-royale prediction.} Early PUBG studies predict final placement from post-match aggregates such as the Kaggle placement data~\cite{kagglePubgFinishPlacement,rokad2019pubgPlacement,sen2021pubgFinalRank} and study factors that influence rank prediction~\cite{lee2025pubgRankFactors}; such inputs are complete only after the match. Rating-system evaluations~\cite{dehpanah2020freeForAllRating,dehpanah2021teamBattleRoyaleRating} likewise target ranking rather than in-match risk. Sagong, Woo, and Kim~\cite{sagong2025hunterCamper} are the closest in-match work: their unit is the player in solo matches only (duo and squad are excluded, citing the role of team strategy), the label is survival into the next half-phase (19 stages) with a winner label at the last stage, eight per-phase accumulated features (FightScore and ActiveScore are unweighted sums) feed random forest, logistic regression, and XGBoost classifiers, and the only signal about other players is a count of players within 100 m. Their data are one week of confidential live-mode logs (7,719 matches); hazard or Cox-type methods are not used, and team-coordination variables, sequence models, and other periods are listed as future work. Sagong and Kim~\cite{sagong2025survivalSequences} feed per-phase strategy sequences to recurrent models to predict the winner.

KRAFTON AI released \texttt{pubg-winprob}~\cite{krafton_pubg_winprob}, a code repository for real-time win-probability prediction in PUBG esports matches: a Transformer applies self-attention across up to 16 squads at sampled time points, counts enemies and teammates within 150 m, and has survival-time, Cox-hazard, and winner heads. The README refers to an accompanying paper, but we located no paper record as of October 2026, so we cite the software and describe only what the code shows. The code includes an attention-free encoder variant (\texttt{--encoder mlp}), so our RQ2 re-examines that mechanism under a different target (next-phase hazard), different data (public-API matches), and a different loss. We run the code as an adapted implementation on our task, not as a reproduction, since its data, splits, and checkpoints are not released.

\emph{Esports prediction and interaction graphs.} Win prediction has been studied for MOBA games~\cite{hodge2017mobaWinPrediction,yang2020honorKingsRealtime,junior2023lolRealtime}, and death prediction in Dota~2~\cite{katona2019dotaDeath} has a short-horizon target like next-phase elimination. Graph models encode relations between teams or players~\cite{bisberg2022gcnWp,xenopoulos2021gnnSports}, and HIGFormer~\cite{wang2025higformer} builds a player--team heterogeneous interaction graph for soccer outcomes. Whether history helps beyond the current state depends on the setting: a 15-step GRU improved on a single frame in Dota~2 death prediction while 3- and 30-step windows did not~\cite{ringer2023timeToDie2}, and an LSTM over traces of length 10 improved most metrics over length 1 in ice hockey~\cite{liu2018hockeyIJCAI}.

\emph{Survival and attention.} We cast elimination as a discrete-time hazard whose intervals are safe-zone phases~\cite{allison1982discrete,singer1993discrete}, evaluated with proper scores (log-loss and Brier); neural discrete-time models include~\cite{gensheimer2019nnetsurvival,lee2018deephit,lee2020dynamicdeephit}, and we use the plain hazard cross-entropy. Factorised attention along time and agent axes is common in multi-agent trajectory modelling~\cite{ngiam2022scenetransformer,capellera2024transportmer}, and Graphormer~\cite{ying2021graphormer} adds graph structure as additive attention biases; we use these mechanisms as switches in one backbone to test information axes. Within our search we did not find a battle-royale model that represents the shrinking safe zone as an explicit object node.
