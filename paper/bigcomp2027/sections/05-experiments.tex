\section{Experimental Protocol}\label{sec:experiments}

\emph{Setup.} One model is trained per cohort and arm, with no pooling across cohorts. All neural arms share one loss (Section~\ref{sec:method}) and one training setup: $d=128$, $H=4$ heads, $L=2$ blocks, feed-forward width $4d$, dropout 0.1, AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), a cosine schedule~\cite{loshchilov2017sgdr}, gradient clipping at 5.0, batches of 8 matches, and at most 12 epochs with early stopping (patience 3) in PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch}. Features are z-scored on the training tokens and missing values are set to zero afterwards. The checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is evaluated once on the test split. The HGB references use histogram gradient boosting~\cite{friedman2001gbm,pedregosa2011sklearn} (200 iterations, learning rate 0.06, $\ell_2$ penalty 0.01, no early stopping, fixed seed and row order), which handles missing values natively. Every contrast uses seed 42; seeds 13 and 2026 are added for M00, M11, M11R, M11R-shuf, and M11RZ in Erangel and Taego TPP squad and enter only as a seed-to-seed standard deviation (at most 0.0014 AUC). In the same two cohorts we also run M11R$-r$ for $r\in\{$prox, combat, copress$\}$ at seed 42 (combat drops both directions). Code and split files: \todo{anonymous repository link or statement}.

\emph{External baselines (exploratory).} KRAFTON-tf and KRAFTON-mlp run the official \texttt{pubg-winprob} code (commit 99ed9d1) adapted to our task: inputs are rebuilt causally from our telemetry (features that cannot be rebuilt are zero-filled), audited bugs are fixed (Cox sign, padding mask, 16-team cap, a 55/57-dimension mismatch), the loss and hyperparameters follow the configuration in the released README, training samples 192 time points per match, evaluation uses our phase boundaries, and probabilities come from Platt scaling on the validation set. K2 combines the KRAFTON backbone with our features. SAGONG-xgb, -rf, and -lr re-implement Sagong et al. as described (eight per-phase features, FightScore and ActiveScore, player type, and type-wise survival rates), at the player level in solo cohorts and aggregated to teams in duo and squad cohorts. All baselines use the same cohorts, splits, labels, and evaluation rows (seed 42) and claim no reproduction of the original results.

\emph{Data scale and player layer (exploratory).} On the two largest cohorts we train HGB-cut, M00, M10, M01, and M11R on 300, 600, and 1,200 training matches and on the full training set (seeds 13, 42, 2026; validation and test sets unchanged; 12 epochs at every size), and regress cohort-level effects on $\ln N$ of training matches with random-effects weights. The player-layer arms run in all 12 cohorts at seed 42.

\emph{Metrics.} Hazard metrics are AUC, average precision, negative log-likelihood, and Brier score over all test rows of a cohort. Relative-standing metrics use the standing score (rank head for neural arms, $-\hat\lambda$ for tree and baseline models) within each (match, phase) risk set: the mean Spearman correlation with the final placement, the pooled top-5 AUC, and the mean within-match C-index.

\emph{Statistics.} For each cohort we resample test matches 2,000 times (paired across arms) and take 95\% percentile intervals; cohort differences are pooled by DerSimonian--Laird random effects~\cite{dersimonian1986metaanalysis}, and we report the between-cohort variance $\tau^2$ and the number of cohorts with the pooled sign. The smallest effect sizes of interest (SESOI) are 0.005 for hazard AUC, 0.01 for Spearman, and 0.005 for top-5 AUC and C-index. Each of the five primary contrasts P1--P5 (Table~\ref{tab:primary}) is tested with a pooled one-sided $z$ test ($H_0\colon\Delta\le0$), and Holm's correction~\cite{holm1979simple} covers these five tests only. A primary contrast is a \emph{gain} if its Holm-adjusted $p$ is below 0.05 and its pooled estimate reaches the SESOI, \emph{equivalent} if its pooled 90\% interval lies within $\pm$SESOI (TOST, per contrast, unadjusted), and \emph{inconclusive} otherwise; an effect that is reliable but below the SESOI is called reliable but small. Displayed 95\% intervals are unadjusted. Everything else is exploratory and uncorrected. The recovery ratio $\kappa$ is descriptive and reported only if the lower end of the pooled 95\% interval of its denominator reaches the SESOI. We also report results by match type (official, competitive).

\emph{Pre-specification and integrity.} The arms, primary contrasts, SESOI, and decision rules were fixed in a timestamped commit before the experiments, and the label, clock, and population rules of Section~\ref{sec:problem} were set before the new models were run. The external baselines and the data-scale analysis were added after the primary results, and the player layer ran last, so all three are exploratory. All features and edges use samples taken no later than 10 s before each phase cut on the aligned clock; HGB baselines run without early stopping, with a fixed row order, on the same rows as the neural arms; all arms and baselines are scored on the same cases of phases 1--7; and the team-axis key mask holds only teams alive at the start of the phase.
